## 0. The one thing

Everyone in this market sells the same deliverables. Accounts, tax, VAT, payroll. The moment we list those, we are in a price comparison with Crunch at GBP 94 a month.

We sell **timing** instead.

> The forms record what happened. The adviser helps decide what happens next.

Every ad, post and page ladders back to that. If a piece of copy would still make sense coming from a GBP 94 a month online filer, it is the wrong copy.

## 1. Who we are selling to

**Not** "small businesses." A UK owner whose company money and personal money have started to collide, so decisions now have consequences.

The core buyer `[A, needs Simon's confirmation]`:

- Limited company director, roughly GBP 150k to GBP 2m turnover
- Takes a mix of salary and dividends, probably has a loan account he does not fully understand
- Has an accountant already. That accountant files correctly and initiates nothing
- Learns his tax bill after the year he could have changed it
- 35 to 55, not guru-adjacent, does not want to be sold to

Four buyer states, each gets its own door:

| State | The trigger | What he privately fears | Door |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growing owner-manager | Profit and cash went up | "I cannot tell what is actually mine" | Owner tax and extraction |
| Under-advised | Accountant only calls at filing time | "I keep finding out too late" | Adviser-led accountancy |
| Internationally mobile | Moving, returning, earning abroad | "Two advisers, two different answers" | UK and international |
| Exit-minded | Sale possible in 1 to 5 years | "It is worth nothing without me" | Exit readiness |

Qualify on **decision complexity**, not turnover. A GBP 100k consultancy with foreign income and a director at the GBP 100k taper needs more judgment than a clean GBP 500k domestic business.

Who we turn away: lowest-fee shoppers, anyone asking for a zero-tax scheme, anyone who will not hand over records until filing week.

## 2. How the buyer buys, and what it means for targeting

Some of this is drawn from a parallel study we ran in another B2B service market, where the buyer was also a business decision-maker buying a licensed professional service. That market produced almost exactly the same channel map as this one, which is why the conclusions below are held with some confidence rather than as guesses.

### The currency is risk, not price

Price matters, and unlike some B2B buyers this one is spending his own money, so it matters more here than in most markets. But it is not the axis the decision turns on. The question behind his question is whether the problem goes away and stays away, and whether he will be caught out.

That means SRJ is not selling accounts, tax and VAT. It is selling the removal of a specific, nameable bad outcome. Vague reassurance does not work. Name the outcome:

- the January bill nobody warned him about, after the money has been spent
- a penalty for something he assumed his accountant was handling
- a dividend taken that quietly cost him his personal allowance
- a relief lost because the planning happened after heads of terms were signed
- a buyer at diligence discounting the business for something visible in the accounts two years earlier
- an HMRC enquiry landing with records that do not support the position

"Peace of mind" is what he calls the result. It is a bad headline because it names no outcome.

### He checks before he calls, and the loss is silent

Before making contact he will look for the professional register entry, reviews, whether anyone like him is a client, and whether the person named on the site is the person he will actually deal with. Any friction there loses the enquiry without ever producing an enquiry, so there is nothing in the data to learn from.

This is why the proof work outranks the advertising. It is not a nice-to-have that improves conversion, it is the gate that decides whether the visit becomes a conversation.

**SRJ's position today:** no verifiable public review count, CIOT and ICAEW record links unconfirmed, one testimonial with an attribution problem, no case studies. Every competitor of consequence leads with proof.

### He is slow to switch, and that cuts both ways

He buys continuously, keeps the same adviser for years, and switching carries admin, awkwardness and the fear of something being lost between firms. So he is expensive to win and extremely valuable once won.

Two consequences. First, the marketing has to be patient, and a lead is worth far more than a single year's fee, which changes what an acceptable cost per enquiry looks like. Second, the switching objection has to be answered before he raises it: clearance, records, software access, opening balances, missing-work review, and any catch-up fee, all stated plainly. See section 1.

### He assumes he is being overcharged

The buyer language is explicit: "I do not know what I am paying for", "am I being rinsed", "Xero is doing 90% of the heavy lifting". His default assumption is that he is paying professional rates for standardised work.

The counter-intuitive consequence: **transparency is a competitive weapon here**, precisely because he expects to be fleeced. Publishing what changes the fee, what is included, and what is project-priced does more for trust than any claim about quality.

### The firm that writes the scope controls the comparison

The strongest version of that idea: when a buyer collects competing quotes, whoever supplies the scope decides what the others are judged against. A bare fee gets compared on price. An itemised scope, with what it covers, when reviews happen, what is excluded and what it costs, becomes the template.

**Actionable for SRJ:** publish what a proper engagement actually includes, as a list he can take away. He will hold the next accountant to it. Nobody else in this market is doing that.

### He cannot be targeted, so the copy has to do the qualifying

"UK company director, GBP 200k or more turnover, with extraction, international or exit complexity" is not an audience that can be selected on any ad platform. There is no job-title targeting that finds him reliably.

So the qualifying happens in the first line of the ad, or the budget leaks to people who will never buy. This is exactly what the one competitor running a real funnel does: he opens with "Ever taken dividends throughout the year, and then got hit with a massive tax bill you didn't see coming?" That sentence is the targeting. Anyone it does not describe scrolls past, which is the point.

Every ad in section 6 has to open by naming a situation, never a service.

### Response failure is the product failing

If the promise is advice in time, then a slow reply is not poor admin, it is the product not working. This is not hypothetical for SRJ: the contact form was silently dead for roughly a month on an expired email key, and every enquiry in that window was lost.

Lead handling gets fixed and tested before any traffic is bought. An enquiry that arrives and sits is more damaging than one that never arrives, because he has already decided SRJ is the answer and then been shown otherwise.

### Capacity is the real ceiling

Simon is the capacity. Five extra clients a month into a one-adviser practice produces missed deadlines, rushed reviews and the exact retrospective service the positioning attacks.

The honest conclusion, and it shapes everything: **sell higher-value work, not more work.** That is an independent argument for leading with international and exit rather than volume compliance switching, separate from the fact that nobody is advertising those doors.

### One question to ask existing clients

There is likely a fee level at which he stops deciding on the spot and starts collecting comparison quotes. Below it, trust and speed win and he can say yes on a call. Above it, the decision slows down and becomes a comparison.

Nobody knows that number for this market yet. Ask five clients what it was for them. It would tell us how to package and stage the paid work.

## 3. Voice of the customer

Their words, from real threads. Use these near-verbatim in headlines and ad copy. Sources are UK subreddits captured Aug 2026 `[F]`.

### What they say about being under-advised (strongest material)

- "I find out after year-end."
- "I want to know in the same tax year."
- "Never any sort of feedback or planning or proactive advice." (owner, low seven-figure turnover, said his accounts were *correct*)
- "I do have an accountant, however I still have penalties for late submissions. I thought having an accountant would make all of this much easier but I still feel responsible."
- "Nobody owns my account."

### About fees and scope

- "I do not know what I am paying for."
- "I do most of it in the portal."
- "Am I being rinsed?" (on a GBP 340 a month quote, one-director company, ~GBP 100k turnover)
- "Xero is doing 90% of the heavy lifting."

### About service and process

- "Tell me what you need and when."
- The accountant who contacted a client days before a VAT deadline and caused a panic. What the buyer wanted: statements, receipts, deadlines and decisions organised in advance.
- Phone access replaced by a ticketing system, repeated document requests, changing contacts.

### About switching (this is the objection that kills deals)

- "Will the old accountant hold things up?"
- Fears: professional clearance being difficult, losing records and software access, exit fees, catch-up fees, and that the new firm will be no different.

### About international

- "I am getting conflicting advice."
- "I want it above board and legal." (director moving to Dubai, getting contradictory answers from a UK accountant and a UAE formation agent)

### About exit

- "What is the business worth without me?"
- "I feel like I bought myself a job, not a business. Everything still depends on me."
- "When I go to sell my business or have an audit done, it shows me earning cash. You're doing the right thing." (on keeping clean books)

### About tax burden (use with care, do not turn into HMRC-bashing)

- "Effectively taking 60p of every GBP 1 I earn over GBP 50k."
- "To pay 20% corporation tax and then 35% on any dividends? Wtf."
- "Taxes is making my business not even worth operating anymore. How do you guys cope?"

### The four emotional jobs

Control. Timeliness. Translation. Ownership. "Peace of mind" is what they call the result, so it is a bad headline. Show what produces it: known reserves, a booked next review, direct answers, clear responsibilities.

### VOC gap to close

The old sheet had ~700 quotes. This has about 25, from 8 threads, and none from SRJ's own funnel: the `leads` table holds **1 row**. Sales calls are the fix. Record trigger, fear, objection and the words used for the next 20 enquiries, then this section gets rewritten from real buyers.

## 4. Words

### Use

- "before the money moves"
- "know what is actually yours"
- "the year-end meeting is too late"
- "one view of the company and you"
- "while you still have a choice"
- "we will tell you in the same tax year"
- "you will speak to Simon, not a ticket"
- "know what still follows you from the UK" (international)
- "the exit starts in the books" (exit)

### Never use

bespoke solutions, trusted partner, comprehensive services, stress-free accounting, maximise your wealth, tax made simple, we care about your business, one-stop shop, and "proactive" with no schedule attached. If we say proactive, the next sentence names the cadence.

### Regulated brand guardrails (CIOT, ICAEW, ASA)

- Verbs are **model, assess, identify, plan, coordinate**. Never guarantee a saving, a relief or a residence outcome.
- No figure in an ad without a source. Every rate cites gov.uk and gets checked before it ships.
- Never "pay no tax", never "HMRC will never know", never a named scheme.
- No attacking competitors by name. Show the structural difference instead.
- International: SRJ owns the UK analysis. Never imply we give UAE legal or regulatory advice.
- Exit: say "exit readiness" or "tax planning before a sale". Not M&A, not valuation, not negotiation.

### Needs evidence before it can ship `[A]`

Exact savings figures. Client counts. Years in practice. Response times. "Specialist" in anything. The PwC role and dates. Review ratings. CIOT and ICAEW logo permission. Whether "direct Simon access" is true at every fee level.

### Where SRJ wins, and where it does not

**Cannot win on:** the timing argument, the "before the money moves" wedge, or a leak-audit offer. All taken, all running.

**Can win on:**

1. **Credential.** He is FCCA and an ACCA-registered practice. SRJ is **Chartered Tax Adviser plus Chartered Accountant plus former PwC.** For a director weighing who to trust with a residence or exit question, that is a real gap and it is the one thing he cannot answer
2. **The two empty doors.** He advertises extraction and corporation tax only. Nobody advertises exit readiness or UK international owner tax
3. **Depth of question.** His funnel tops out at salary and dividends. It has no answer for a director leaving the UK, or one selling in three years

**Do copy the structure:** a tool that returns a number about the reader's own money, a wide free top-of-funnel gate, a stated turnover qualifier on the booking page, and one tested winner rather than twenty untested creatives.

### The verdict on paid

Paid is not where this category is won, and there is no evidence anyone is winning with it.

| Firm | Live paid ads | What they actually win on |
|---|---|---|
| Sidekick | 0 | Accumulated content and benchmarks |
| The Wow Company | 0 | Community and benchmarking data |
| Protax | 0 | Visible prices, named professional, reviews |
| Alto | 0 | Precise vertical copy |
| DC Accountants | 19, 8 barely delivering | A calculator that returns a number about your own money |
| TaxAssist | ~300 | Franchise scale, and recruiting franchisees too |
| Crunch | 22 | Price and volume |

**Every firm competing for SRJ's exact client runs zero paid.** The two advertising at scale are a franchise network and a low-price online filer, neither of which is SRJ's game.

An empty channel has two readings and both deserve equal weight. The optimistic one is white space: no auction to lose, and copy aimed at this buyer would stand out completely. The pessimistic one is that the channel is empty because it has already been tested and does not work, since this buyer cannot be targeted, buys on relationships and incumbency, and is triggered by a deadline or a shock rather than by scrolling. Treat any paid social as a test with a kill date, never as a channel.

Read the pattern properly: what wins is not a channel, it is an **asset**. Every firm getting traction owns something that produces a number or a comparison about the reader's own business. Ads are only delivery for that asset. DC Accountants is not winning with ads, he is testing ads to distribute a calculator.

**So: no paid spend yet.** Four reasons, all evidenced:

1. **No proof assets.** Zero verifiable public reviews, while every competitor leads with theirs. Ads amplify a funnel, they do not repair one
2. **The offer at the end is broken.** The scorecard still says agency
3. **No tracking.** No pixel, no conversion event, so the spend cannot even be attributed
4. **Category reality.** Accountancy is a trust-led purchase with switching costs. Paid social interrupts, it does not manufacture that intent

Spending GBP 700 to GBP 1,000 a month today loses the money and teaches nothing, because with no pixel there is nothing to learn from.

### The alternative, in priority order

1. **Reviews. Costs nothing.** Ask every current client this month. Highest return of anything here, and it closes the one gap competitors are actively beating SRJ with
2. **LinkedIn organic from Simon's spines. Costs nothing.** The buyer is there, it compounds, it needs no infrastructure. Sections 7 and 8 cover it
3. **Build the calculator.** The real unlock, and the thing to fund before any media. Not a scorecard that returns a score, a calculator that returns a number about the reader's own money. Simon's CTA plus ex-PwC credential means a residence or an exit version of it is something no competitor can match, and nobody is advertising those doors at all
4. **Three case studies.** Situation, decision, work, result

Those four are worth more than any budget that could be put behind the current site.

### Then Google Search, not Meta

When paid does start, it starts on Google, and this is the reasoning:

- **The intent already exists.** Somebody typing "moving abroad uk limited company tax" has the problem today. Nothing has to be manufactured
- **It does not depend on the pixel.** Search converts on intent, so it works before the tracking is perfect. Meta without a conversion event optimises for cheap clicks and buys the worst traffic available
- **It is not a crowded auction.** Outside TaxAssist and Crunch, the competitor set bids nothing

- **Budget control.** A few hundred pounds on four exact-match terms is a readable test. The same money on Meta buys reach and no answer

**Start at GBP 300 to GBP 400 a month on the two empty doors only.** International and exit. Add the extraction and switch groups once those two have data.

## 7. Social

### Platforms

**LinkedIn is the channel.** Simon is a named professional and the buyer is a UK company director. Post as Simon, not as a company page. The company page reposts.

**Instagram second**, same content reformatted, mainly for reach and for the younger agency cohort we already have content for.

**Cadence is set by Simon's supply, not by a number on a calendar.** We post as many as he sends. He is the only source of the spine, so his throughput is the real ceiling and there is no point pretending otherwise.

What that means in practice: he logs a line whenever a client asks him something, and every one of those becomes a post. If that is three in a week, we run three. If it is six, we run six. The mix in the table below holds at any volume, and the sign-off standard never moves.

### Post type mix

Out of every 10 posts:

| # | Type | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Informational, threshold or deadline led** | The workhorse. One rule, one trap, one action. This is Simon's caption engine, already written |
| 2 | **Myth correction** | "You were told X. X is a starting point, not the answer." Highest engagement, because it makes people check their own setup |
| 1 | **Carousel** | Teach-down of something with steps or a sequence. Saves and shares |
| 1 | **Proof** | A client situation, decision, work, result. Anonymised until permission exists |
| 1 | **Simon's view** | An opinion on a rule change. This is the only post type a competitor cannot copy |
| 1 | **Light or human** | Keeps the feed from reading like a compliance bulletin |

### Type by type, with examples

**Informational.** One idea per post. Open with the threshold, not the preamble. Simon's existing captions are already the right shape:
> If your agency has employees, do not miss the GBP 10,500 National Insurance relief. There is one trap that stops most one-person companies claiming it.

Structure: the number, who it applies to, the trap, the action, the date. Five lines.

**Myth correction.** The format is "you were told, here is what it depends on."
> "Small salary plus dividends" is not tax advice. It is a starting pattern.
> What actually changes the answer: your other income, whether the Employment Allowance is available, company profit, your spouse's position, pension plans, and how much cash you need.
> Same turnover, two directors, completely different right answers.

**Carousel.** Six to eight slides. Slide 1 is a promise, not a title. Last slide is one action, not a hard sell. Three structures that work:
- *The sequence:* "Leaving the UK with a UK company: the order to do it in." One decision per slide.
- *The threshold map:* "Five thresholds that change what you keep." GBP 50k, GBP 90k, GBP 100k, GBP 125,140, the pension taper. One per slide, what changes, what to do.
- *The before and after:* "What a buyer sees in your accounts." Slide per line item, what it looks like now, what it should look like 18 months out.

**Proof.** Situation, decision, work, result, in four lines. No figure without permission.
> A director was about to take a GBP 30k dividend in March. His adjusted net income was already close to GBP 100k.
> We modelled it against a pension contribution and splitting the timing across the tax year.
> The dividend still happened. The personal allowance survived.

**Simon's view.** Take a position on something recent, with the reasoning visible. Business Relief being capped at GBP 2.5m from April 2026, BADR at 18%, MTD landing for landlords. This is where the CTA credential earns its keep. One a week, and it can be less polished than the rest.

**Light or human.** This is the one to get right, because a regulated practice gets it wrong in a specific way.

Safe: the shared absurdity of admin. Receipts in a shoebox. The client who calls the accounts "the paperwork". Companies House at 11:58pm. Being the only person at the barbecue who has opinions about dividend timing. Self-deprecation about being an accountant at parties.

Not safe, ever: jokes about avoiding tax, jokes about HMRC being stupid or beatable, jokes about a client's ignorance, jokes that imply we bend rules, and anything self-deprecating about competence or deadlines. Simon sells judgment. A joke that costs credibility is expensive.

Test: would this be fine if a CIOT reviewer and a prospect both read it? If not, do not post it.

### The rotation

There is no fixed weekly grid, because the volume is whatever Simon sends. Run a rotation instead and take the next type in order as each spine arrives:

**Informational, informational, myth correction, view, informational, proof, carousel, light.** Then repeat.

That keeps the mix roughly at the table above whether he sends three in a week or seven, and it means nobody has to decide what today's post should be. If two hard-claim spines land back to back, run both and let the rotation slip. The mix matters over a month, not over a week.

Sequence it against the tax year, not at random. Pre-year-end options in Jan to Mar. Post-April changes in April. VAT threshold and rolling turnover when the deregistration question comes up. MTD ahead of April 2027. Exit content year-round, since it has no deadline.

Comments matter more than posts. A director who asks a question in the comments and gets a real answer is worth more than reach.

## 8. The content engine

One idea, three surfaces, four gates. This is how a post actually gets made.

### The division of labour

| Who | Owns | Does not touch |
|---|---|---|
| Simon | Every number, rule, date, threshold and technical claim. The professional position. Final sign-off | Structure, headline, hook, sequencing |
| Hazem | Structure, hook, headline, persuasion, the cut, channel formatting | Any tax fact, any figure, any claim about what a rule does |

This split is not a preference, it is the liability line. Simon is the only person who can approve a tax statement under his CIOT and ICAEW obligations. Copy that reads well and is wrong is worse than copy that reads badly and is right.

### The order (facts before polish)

The instinct is to write it, then have Simon check it. That wastes the writing, because a corrected fact usually changes the argument, and then the copy gets rebuilt anyway.

Run it the other way:

1. **Simon drops the spine.** The rule, the number, the trap, the date, the source, and one line of his own view. Telegram, voice note, three sentences, whatever is fastest. He already does this, it is what the 31 queued posts were built from
2. **Draft.** Structure, hook, headline, sequencing, examples. Facts are carried across untouched, and anything uncertain is flagged in the draft rather than smoothed over
3. **Copy review.** Against this sheet: does it use their words, does it avoid the banned list, does it ladder to timing rather than deliverables
4. **Simon signs off once.** Numbers, rules, dates, sources, and whether he is happy to have said it. One gate, not two. Logged with the date and the sources checked
5. **Ship and cut.** Blog goes into the queue, then the LinkedIn post and the video come off the finished piece

Rule: if a fact is unverified at step 4, it comes out. It does not ship with a hedge.

### One idea, three surfaces

The blog is the master. The other two are cuts of it, not translations of it.

| Surface | Length | What it takes from the blog | Who fronts it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blog | 600 to 900 words | The whole argument, the sources, the caveats | Simon, bylined |
| LinkedIn | 120 to 200 words | The single sharpest point plus his view. Not a summary | Simon's profile |
| Instagram or Reels | 30 to 45 seconds | One hook, one number, one action | Simon on camera |

The mistake to avoid: compressing 900 words into a 40 second video. It becomes a list nobody finishes. Pick the one line that makes someone check their own setup, and build the video on that alone. Everything else is what the blog is for.

### Two things that will break this

**Simon is a solo practitioner.** If every post needs him to record a video, this stops in week two. So batch: **one recording session a month, 8 to 10 videos in a sitting**, shot against the same background from a shortlist of hooks already approved. Writing and posting stay weekly, filming does not.

**Volume comes from Simon's supply, so protect the supply.** Every client question he answers in a week is a post, and he only has to log one line when it happens. That is the engine. Nothing else generates ideas at the rate this needs.

The surfaces do not run at the same rate. LinkedIn text is cheap for him and video is expensive, so video follows the posts that landed rather than accompanying all of them: **batch 4 to 6 videos a month** off the best performers, in one sitting.

### Definition of done

A post is finished when all five are true:

- Every figure traced to gov.uk and the link checked live
- Simon's sign-off logged with the date, in `docs/simon-sign-off-blog-posts.md`
- No phrase from the banned list in section 4
- One action for the reader, not three
- The LinkedIn cut and the video hook chosen at the same time, not retrofitted a week later

### What this gets us

Three channels off one piece of thinking, with the credential visible on all three. Competitors publishing volume without a named Chartered Tax Adviser behind it cannot copy the sign-off, and that is the part that compounds.
