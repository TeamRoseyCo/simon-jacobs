# Competitor keyword and page-architecture gap analysis, August 2026

Method: direct sitemap extraction for all 15 domains in the brief, followed by
title / H1 / H2 / word-count retrieval on 22 head-to-head landing pages, plus
whois registration dates and on-page trust-signal scraping. Every count below
is a count of real URLs pulled from a live sitemap on 4 Aug 2026, not an
estimate. No search volume data was available, so nothing here is ranked by
volume. Rankings are by evidence type, stated each time.

**All 15 sitemaps were reachable. Nothing was blocked.** Raw URL sets were
extracted from flat sitemaps (Alto, Wow, Sidekick, COPA, David Howard) and from
recursive sitemap indexes (the rest, up to 13 sub-sitemaps each).

---

## Headline finding, before the detail

The competitive set is not what `gate-1-competitor-study.md` described in June.
Two things have changed, and one of them is serious.

1. **Alto Accounting is the real threat and it did not exist 20 months ago.**
   Domain registered **4 Jan 2025**. It now has **322 URLs**: 16 niche vertical
   pages, 19 city pages, 227 insight articles, 12 calculators, 10 comparison
   pages, a pricing page and an editorial policy. SRJ's domain was registered
   **2 Mar 2026**. Alto is only about 14 months older than SRJ. Its advantage is
   **architecture and publishing volume, not domain age**. That is both the bad
   news and the good news.

2. **Alto is targeting SRJ's exact query set, including the odd ones.** It has
   `/locations/wallington` (SRJ's GSC query "accountant for digital agencies
   wallington", 8 impressions), plus ~20 insight pages on UK-to-Dubai / UAE /
   statutory residence, the second half of SRJ's GSC impression profile. This
   is not a coincidental overlap. It is the same ICP and the same terms.

3. **The "exit angle is wide open" conclusion in the June study is now wrong.**
   Alto has 9 exit-specific pages (BADR rate change 2026, EBITDA add-backs,
   quality of earnings, vendor due diligence, EMI, EOT, recurring revenue and
   valuation, plus a BADR calculator). Sidekick has agency exit strategy,
   acquisition due diligence and per-vertical valuation pages. Rise has 12.
   The exit lane is now contested. It is still SRJ's best *credential* fit, but
   it is no longer empty and should not be planned as if it is.

## Per-competitor summary

### Alto Accounting, alto-accounting.com
**The one to study.** Domain 4 Jan 2025. 322 URLs.

- **Targets:** `/agency-accountant` is the hub (1,689 words, title "Marketing &
  Creative Agency Accountants UK | from £200"). Then 16 `/accounting/<vertical>`
  pages: marketing agencies, advertising agencies, branding agencies,
  communications-pr-agencies, creative studios, video production, web design,
  influencers, OnlyFans creators, Shopify sellers, freelancers. Plus a second
  parallel set at `/for/` (creative studios, digital marketing agencies,
  photography and production).
- **Architecture:** service × vertical × city. Three city sub-pages hang off
  verticals (`/accounting/branding-agencies/bristol`,
  `/accounting/creative-studios/manchester`, `/accounting/marketing-agencies/birmingham`),
  and 19 standalone `/locations/` pages, heavily South-West London: Wallington,
  Sutton, Croydon, Clapham, Putney, Richmond, Tooting, Twickenham, Wimbledon,
  Kingston, Shoreditch, Surrey, plus Birmingham, Bristol, Edinburgh, Leeds,
  London, Manchester.
- **Blog depth:** 227 insight pages. Roughly a third are agency-operational
  (utilisation rate, lock-up days, debtor/creditor days, WIP, accrued vs
  deferred income, ad spend reconciliation, principal vs agent revenue,
  FRS 102 revenue recognition, blended rate pricing, overhead recovery,
  scope creep). Roughly 20 are UK expat / Dubai / UAE. The rest is UK tax
  compliance news at high cadence (2026 rate changes, Companies House ECCTA,
  MTD ITSA, IR35 2026).
- **Distinctive assets SRJ has no equivalent of:** 12 free calculators
  (profitability, runway, LTV:CAC, cash-flow forecaster, BADR, R&D, IR35 status
  checker, SRT day counter, director pension, mileage, salary, VAT threshold);
  an `/agency-tax-health-check`; `/agency-benchmarks` and
  `/creative-agency-benchmarks`; an `/agency-tax-calendar`; and **10 comparison
  pages** including `/compare/best-agency-accountants-uk`,
  `/compare/agile-accountants-alternative`, `/compare/crunch-accounting-alternative`,
  `/compare/accountant-vs-bookkeeper-agencies`.
- **Trust signals:** **ACCA registered practice only.** No ICAEW, no CIOT, no
  CTA. Xero Certified. **35 reviews** surfaced on the hub page. Registered
  office 66 Paul Street, London EC2A 4NA. Transparent pricing "from £200".
- **How beatable:** **Very beatable on page quality and on credential; not
  beatable on breadth without sustained publishing.** Its 16 vertical pages are
  **template clones of ~870 to 920 words** with byte-identical H2s across every
  vertical ("Most accountants treat every business the same." / "What we hear
  from *X* on the first call." / "Reading and tools for *X*."). The Wallington
  page is **313 words**. These are thin pages ranking on site-level topical
  mass and internal linking, not on individual page merit. **That thinness is
  the single most useful fact in this report: the terms are winnable.** Alto
  also has no chartered tax credential at all, which is SRJ's whole basis.

### Sidekick Accounting, sidekickaccounting.co.uk
Domain 24 Oct 2022. **1,151 URLs.** The largest content operation in the set.

- **Targets:** 10 `/sectors/` pages, all pure agency, no generalist verticals:
  digital marketing, creative, branding, PR, SEO, PPC, social media marketing,
  email marketing, performance marketing, influencer marketing.
- **Architecture:** a **programmatic vertical × topic matrix**. 1,128 `/insights/`
  pages, produced by crossing ~10 verticals against ~75 finance topics. Token
  frequency confirms it: `agency` 1,022, `marketing` 304, then `pr` 82, `seo` 78,
  `performance` 77, `media` 76, `social` 76, `ppc` 76, `email` 76, `branding` 74,
  `influencer` 71, `ai` 79. The same 75 topics repeat per vertical:
  `<vertical>-cash-flow-forecasting`, `-client-payment-terms`,
  `-acquisition-preparation`, `-audit-preparation-checklist`,
  `-advisory-pricing-model`, `-valuation-methods`, and so on.
- **Blog depth:** the deepest in the set, and **not thin**. A sampled insight
  page (`/insights/pr-agency-cash-flow-forecasting`) is **2,130 words** with 11
  H2s all written as questions ("What is cash flow forecasting for a PR agency?",
  "How do you forecast unpredictable retainer income?") and a "Questions agency
  owners ask" block. This is deliberate answer-engine optimisation and it is
  well executed.
- **Sector pages:** ~1,410 to 1,470 words, genuinely varied per vertical, with a
  results block and a scorecard CTA.
- **Trust signals:** **ICAEW Chartered Accountants**, "100+ agencies served",
  "5% avg margin improvement*" (asterisked). Agency Profit Score scorecard,
  `/agency-benchmarks`, `/tax-savings-calculator`, `/cfo`. **Still no
  third-party review count anywhere.**
- **How beatable:** **Genuinely strong. The hardest content competitor here.**
  Beatable only on (a) credential, since ICAEW is not CTA, (b) tax depth, since
  the corpus is finance-operational rather than tax-technical, and (c) the total
  absence of public reviews. Do not attempt to out-publish Sidekick.

### The Wow Company, thewowcompany.com
468 URLs. `/agencies` hub is only **718 words**.

- **Targets:** one vertical, "agencies", plus programme pages:
  `/agency-profit-programme`, `/agency-leader-roundtables`, `/agency-matchmaker`,
  `/agency-bites`, `/agency-valuation-email-series`, `/agencies-how-to-grow-beyond-5m`.
- **Architecture:** almost no SEO landing-page layer. It is a community and data
  brand. **BenchPress** benchmarking runs unbroken from 2012 to present
  (`/blog/agency/agency-benchmarking-2012` through 2019 and onward) plus a
  BenchPress hub with video benchmark reports.
- **Blog depth:** large but old. Much of the agency blog is 2012 to 2019 era.
- **How beatable:** **Old, not strong, on search.** Its moat is the 14-year
  benchmark dataset and the community, neither of which is a keyword. It is
  ranking on age and links, not on covering terms SRJ is missing. Do not model
  page architecture on Wow.

### Raedan, raedan.co.uk
Domain 12 Feb 2012. 166 URLs. **The closest structural analogue to SRJ.**

- **Targets:** the widest set of `accountants-for-<X>` slugs in the set:
  marketing agencies, PR agencies, advertising agencies, creative agencies,
  creatives, agencies, the creative industry, artists, photographers, gaming
  industry, small business. Plus `accounting-for-marketing-agencies` and
  `accounting-for-video-production`.
- **Architecture:** flat, one page per term, no city layer, no tools.
  **It has both `/accountants-for-marketing-agencies/` and
  `/accounting-for-marketing-agencies/`, self-cannibalising duplicates**, which
  is exactly the risk SRJ is currently running with four near-identical pages.
- **Blog depth:** ~140 posts but the archive is stale and generic. Large
  Coronavirus block, Budget 2018, Budget 2020. Recent posts are thin listicles.
- **Page quality:** vertical pages **863 to 980 words**, and the templating is
  visible in the copy ("Expert accounting for pr agencies Services" with broken
  capitalisation, "Simple *X* Agency Accounting Tips", "We Know What Agencies
  Want, We Are One Ourselves" repeated verbatim).
- **Trust signals:** none surfaced. No review count, no chartered badge found.
- **How beatable:** **Old, not strong.** 14-year domain plus one page per term.
  Content quality is below what SRJ already ships. High-priority target.

## The 10 highest-value gaps, ranked

Ranked by strength of evidence, not by guessed volume. Tier A means there is
direct GSC demand evidence *and* a structural opening. Tier B means structural
opening only.

**Tier A: demonstrated demand plus a real opening**

1. **A dedicated "accountants for media agencies" page.**
   This is SRJ's single highest-impression query (20 impressions, position 71.6)
   and **not one of the 14 competitors has a dedicated page for it.** Sidekick
   has 76 pages using the word "media" but every one is *social* media. SRLV is
   the only firm with genuine media-agency credibility and it files that under
   `/industries/advertising-marketing/` and `/industries/entertainment/`. This is
   the cleanest term-to-vacancy match in the entire dataset: proven impressions,
   zero dedicated competition, and it sits inside SRJ's existing ICP. Nothing
   else in this report is this unambiguous.

2. **A UK-to-Dubai / UAE tax planning money page for agency owners.**
   Four SRJ GSC queries totalling ~41 impressions ("uk to dubai tax advice" 19,
   "uk to uae tax planning" 16, "tax consultant for british expats dubai" 6) and
   SRJ has **no landing page at all**, only three blog posts. Every competitor
   covering Dubai is mis-angled: **Rise's ~40 pages are business-*setup* intent**
   (formation, visas, licences, corporate tax registration), **Alto's ~20 are
   generic expat pages** with no agency framing, and **Smooth's are influencer
   and general-SME**. Nobody owns "UK agency owner relocating to Dubai, what
   happens to my UK tax". A **CTA** is the correct professional for exactly this
   question, and Alto, Agile, Rise and Smooth have no chartered tax credential
   between them. This is the strongest *defensible* lane in the report, as
   opposed to the most obvious one.

3. **Reviews.** Not a keyword, but the gap table makes it unavoidable: the four
   firms with visible review counts (Agile 137, A&C 115, Smooth 103, Alto 35)
   are the four firms ranking on these terms, and Agile embeds its 137 reviews
   *inside* the marketing-agency landing page. SRJ has zero. Every content
   recommendation below is throttled by this. The June study called it the
   number one build item; it is still the number one build item, and it is now
   the only thing separating SRJ from Alto, which is 14 months older with no
   chartered credential.

4. **Free calculators.** 34 across four competitors, and Alto's 12 are its main
   link-earning and email-capture layer. SRJ has a `/scorecard` and nothing
   else. The highest-fit builds given the CTA positioning: a **BADR / exit tax
   calculator** (Alto has one, and this is Simon's actual expertise), a
   **statutory residence test day counter** (supports gap 2, Alto has one), and
   an **agency profitability or runway calculator**. These are also the most
   likely SRJ pages to attract natural links, which addresses the authority
   constraint rather than the relevance one.

**Tier B: structural opening, no GSC evidence yet**

5. **A "best accountants for UK marketing agencies" comparison page.**
   Alto runs 10 of these and Rise's 2,931-word listicle is how it ranks at all.
   This is the format that wins the buyer-comparison query and it is the format
   most likely to get cited by ChatGPT, which matters given SRJ's AEO goals. Two
   caveats: **the ASA and CIOT constraints mean a self-serving "we're the best"
   listicle is not available to SRJ.** The compliant version is Alto's actual
   framing, "How to Find the Best Accountant for Your UK Agency" (1,362 words,
   H2s: what makes a good agency accountant, key questions to ask, red flags,
   what to expect from pricing, how to switch). SRJ already has
   `/blog/how-to-choose-a-tax-adviser-for-your-marketing-agency`. **Promote it
   to a money page and expand it.** This is the cheapest high-value move here.

6. **Agency operational finance content, framed as tax.** Alto has ~70 pages and
   Sidekick ~900 on utilisation rates, lock-up days, WIP, debtor days,
   accrued vs deferred income, revenue recognition, retainer pricing, ad spend
   reconciliation, principal vs agent. **SRJ has effectively none.** This is the
   topical mass that makes Alto's thin 890-word vertical pages rank. SRJ should
   not try to match the volume, but the tax-adjacent subset is both winnable and
   on-brand: **principal vs agent revenue recognition and its VAT consequences,
   FRS 102 revenue recognition on retainers, ad spend recharges and VAT,
   accrued vs deferred income at year end.** These are genuinely tax-technical
   questions where a CTA outranks an ACCA bookkeeper on merit.

7. **Statutory residence test, split year treatment, FIG regime, overseas
   workday relief.** Only Alto, Sidekick and SRLV touch these. They are the
   supporting cluster that makes gap 2 credible and they are squarely CTA
   territory. Build these as the cluster underneath the Dubai money page rather
   than as standalone posts.

8. **"How to switch accountants / tax advisers".** Alto has two pages plus a
   dedicated `/switch-accountants`. It is a bottom-of-funnel commercial query
   with almost no competition in the set, it is cheap to write, and it converts.

9. **A named case study with a real client name and a real number.** Goodman
   Jones has 24 named client stories; nobody in the agency-specialist group has
   any with figures. The June study already identified this. Note the tension
   with the standing instruction to keep client names vague in public marketing,
   so the realistic version is an anonymised-but-quantified study ("a 
   £1.4m London media agency"), which still beats the entire field.

10. **A pricing or "how our pricing works" page.** Five of 14 publish pricing,
    including all three of the newest and fastest-growing (Alto from £200,
    COPA from £75, Agile's instant quote). This is a decision for Simon, not a
    recommendation. The evidence is only that the firms winning cold search
    traffic publish something, and SRJ publishes nothing.

**Explicitly do not chase:** Making Tax Digital (9 competitors, commodity, and
A&C's 20 posts prove it is a content graveyard), influencers and content
creators (4 competitors, off-ICP), freelancers and sole traders (off-ICP),
Dubai company formation (Rise owns it and it is the wrong intent), and the
SEO/PPC/social sub-verticals (Sidekick's programmatic matrix makes these
uneconomic to contest).

## The direct question: more vertical pages, or consolidate?

The honest answer is **neither as stated. Consolidate the four you have, add
exactly one new vertical page, and spend the freed capacity on the supporting
corpus.** Here is the argument from what competitors actually do.

**The case for more vertical pages, stated fairly.** It is the dominant pattern.
13 of 14 firms run at least one vertical landing page. Two run 10 or more (Alto
16, Sidekick 10) and both are recent, fast-growing and cited by ChatGPT. SRLV
runs 13 industries on a serious chartered practice. If you only counted noses,
the field says build more.

**But the counting is misleading, and here is why.** Look at what the vertical
pages actually are on the two firms running the most of them.

Alto's 16 vertical pages are **template clones**. Marketing agencies 867 words,
branding agencies 923, PR agencies 893, and the H2s are **byte-identical across
all of them**: "Most accountants treat every business the same." / "What we hear
from *X* on the first call." / "Services tailored to *X*." / "Reading and tools
for *X*." Only the noun changes. Its Wallington page is 313 words. These pages
do not rank on their own merit. They rank because they sit on top of **227
insight articles and 12 calculators** that link into them and establish
site-level topical authority. The vertical page is the *harvesting* layer. The
corpus is the *earning* layer.

Sidekick is the same architecture at 10× scale: 10 sector pages of ~1,420 words
sitting on **1,128 insight pages**. The ratio is 113 supporting pages per
vertical page.

**SRJ's ratio is inverted.** Four vertical pages sitting on 22 blog posts, which
is 5.5 supporting pages per vertical page. Alto has 14 per vertical, Sidekick
113. SRJ has already built the harvesting layer and has not built the earning
layer. **Adding media, PR and branding pages would take that ratio from 5.5 to
3.1.** It would replicate Alto's thin template without Alto's corpus underneath
it, which is copying the visible half of a mechanism and omitting the half that
makes it work.

**The second reason to be cautious is cannibalisation, and there is direct
evidence for it in this dataset.** Raedan runs both
`/accountants-for-marketing-agencies/` and `/accounting-for-marketing-agencies/`,
plus `/accountants-for-creatives/` and `/accountants-for-creative-agencies/`, and
`/accountants-for-agencies/` and `/accountants-for-the-creative-industry/`. That
is six pages competing for one intent on a 14-year domain with better links than
SRJ, and it is not winning. SRJ's four money pages are the same failure mode in
miniature: near-identical intent, split signals, and GSC showing all of them
buried at 50 to 83 while Google picks one. **Raedan is the control experiment for
what SRJ is about to do more of.**

**The third reason is the specific diagnosis in the brief.** Position 50 to 83
on topically correct pages is not a relevance failure that another page fixes.
It is an authority failure. David Howard holds rank with a **489-word** page and
A&C with one agency page out of 15 sectors, both on the strength of domain age,
review volume and blog mass. Agile embeds **137 reviews** in its landing page.
Nothing about adding a fifth and sixth near-duplicate 800-word page changes any
of those inputs.

**So, concretely.**

- **Consolidate the four existing near-duplicates into one authoritative hub**
  at the URL Google has already chosen, with the others 301'd or reduced to
  genuinely differentiated deep sections. Target 2,500 to 3,000 words, which
  puts it above Agile's 2,817 and well above Alto's 867. Cover PR, branding,
  advertising and creative as substantial named sections plus FAQ entries inside
  it, not as separate URLs. This concentrates the signal instead of splitting it
  five ways.
- **Add exactly one new vertical page: media agencies.** It earns its exception
  on evidence no other vertical has: 20 real impressions, SRJ's top query, and
  **zero dedicated competitor pages across all 14 firms.** It is not a
  cannibalisation risk because "media agency" is a distinct entity from
  "marketing agency", and it is the one place SRJ can plausibly be the best page
  on the internet within a week.
- **Do not add PR or branding as standalone pages yet.** Three and two
  competitors respectively, both with far larger sites, and both intents are
  adequately served by sections in the hub. Revisit once the hub ranks inside
  the top 20 for its head term, which is the point at which a spin-out has
  something to inherit.
- **Spend the freed capacity on the earning layer**, in this order: the Dubai
  and residence cluster (gaps 2 and 7, where the competition is mis-angled), two
  or three calculators (gap 4, because they earn links, which is the actual
  constraint), the tax-framed operational finance subset (gap 6), and the
  choose-an-adviser and switching money pages (gaps 5 and 8).
- **City pages: not yet, with one narrow exception.** Four firms run 50 between
  them and Alto's Wallington page is 313 words, which tells you these are cheap
  and thin. SRJ has a real GSC signal at "accountant for digital agencies
  wallington" (8 impressions, **position 21, the best position in the whole
  data set**) and Alto has a page for exactly that. That single overlap is worth
  one page. A 19-page location build is not, because SRJ has no local citation
  or Google Business Profile foundation to hang it on, and thin local pages
  without that foundation are the classic way to dilute a small site.

The uncomfortable summary: **the plan to add vertical pages is directionally
normal for the industry but solves the wrong problem for SRJ right now.** The
competitors who win with many vertical pages built the corpus first and the
vertical pages second. SRJ has done it in the opposite order and the GSC data is
what that looks like.

## What I could not verify

- **Search volume for anything.** No volume tool was available. Nothing in this
  report is ranked by volume, and the two rankings that exist are labelled by
  evidence type. Treat "highest-value" as "strongest evidence", not "most
  searches".
- **Actual ranking positions for competitors.** I did not scrape live SERPs, so
  claims that a firm "ranks" for a term come from `gate-1-competitor-study.md`
  (June 2026) or from the structural evidence, not from a fresh SERP check.
  **The claim that "the term is winnable because the page is thin" rests on
  verified word counts plus the June ranking claims, and the June rankings are
  now two months old.** Worth a spot check before acting on gap 1 or the
  Wallington page.
- **Domain authority, backlink counts, referring domains.** No Ahrefs, Moz or
  Semrush access. My "authority not relevance" reasoning is inferred from domain
  age, page count and review count, which are proxies. **This is the weakest
  link in the analysis and it is load-bearing for the consolidate-versus-expand
  argument.** If SRJ has Search Console link data, checking Alto's and Raedan's
  referring-domain counts would either confirm or overturn my conclusion.
- **Review counts are floors, not totals.** I read only what was rendered on the
  specific pages fetched. Alto's 35, Agile's 137, A&C's 115 and Smooth's 103 are
  what those pages surfaced. Firms showing "none" (Raedan, Rise, Sidekick, Wow,
  Foxsmith, David Howard, Goodman Jones, SRLV) may have Google Business Profile
  reviews I did not query, since I did not check GBP directly.
- **COPA's content is invisible to me, not necessarily absent.** Its page
  returned 6 words because it renders client-side. Its 77 sitemap URLs may
  contain substantial content that a JS-executing crawler would see. I judged it
  a non-threat on the basis of the raw HTML only. Google does render JS, so this
  judgement could be wrong.
- **Publication and update dates.** I did not parse lastmod, so "blog depth" is
  page count, not freshness. My "old, not strong" calls on Wow, Raedan and
  Goodman Jones are based on slug content (Coronavirus, Budget 2018,
  benchmarking-2012) rather than systematic date analysis.
- **Alto's 322 pages could be AI-generated at scale.** The identical H2 templates
  across 16 verticals and the 227-article insight library on a 19-month domain
  are consistent with that. I could not confirm it, and it matters: if Alto is
  programmatic and gets hit by a future quality update, the competitive picture
  changes substantially in SRJ's favour. Worth watching rather than acting on.
- **Sidekick's true page count in Google's index.** 1,151 sitemap URLs is not
  1,151 indexed pages. A programmatic matrix of that shape is a common candidate
  for partial indexing, so its effective footprint may be much smaller than the
  sitemap suggests.
- **The ChatGPT citation set could not be reproduced.** I took the six cited
  domains as given from the brief and did not re-run the prompt, so I cannot say
  why Goodman Jones and Leon & Company in particular are cited. Neither has a
  content profile that explains it, which suggests the citations are driven by
  off-site signals I cannot see.
