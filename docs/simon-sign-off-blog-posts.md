### The 60% tax trap, and why agency owners keep hitting it

https://srjinternational.co.uk/blog/the-60-percent-tax-trap

735 words, published 2026-07-16.

Figures and rules stated, please check:

- There is a band of income in the UK where every extra pound is taxed at an effective 60 percent, even though no tax table anywhere lists a 60 percent rate.
- ## What the 60% tax trap actually is It comes from the way the Personal Allowance is withdrawn.
- Everyone gets a tax-free Personal Allowance, but once your income goes over £100,000 you start to lose it: £1 of allowance for every £2 of income above that line, until it is gone entirely at £125,140.
- Losing tax-free allowance while also paying tax on the income itself is what pushes the effective rate on that slice to around 60 percent.
- HMRC sets this out in its guidance on income over £100,000.
- ## Does anyone really pay 60% tax?
- Not as a headline rate, no.
- The published higher rate is 40 percent.

### Selling stocks and shares as an agency owner: the tax, in plain English

https://srjinternational.co.uk/blog/tax-when-you-sell-stocks-and-shares-agency-owner

941 words, published 2026-07-16.

Figures and rules stated, please check:

- You get an annual tax-free allowance, the annual exempt amount, of £3,000 for 2026 to 2027, and that single allowance covers all your gains for the year, so if you have also sold property or crypto it is shared across the lot.
- Above the allowance you pay 18% on gains that sit within the basic rate band and 24% on anything above it, which is where most higher and additional rate taxpayers land.
- If you qualify for Business Asset Disposal Relief, the gain on the sale of your agency is taxed at 18% from 6 April 2026, up to a lifetime limit, rather than at the standard rates.
- The conditions are specific: broadly, you need to have been an employee or officer of the company, hold at least 5% of the shares and voting rights, and the company needs to be trading, all for at least two years before you sell.
- HMRC lists the full conditions under Business Asset Disposal Relief.
- The first £500 of dividends in a year is tax-free, then you pay 10.75%, 35.75%, or 39.35% depending on which tax band the dividend falls into.
- For agency owners this matters twice over, because the dividends you take out of your own company use the same allowance and the same rates as dividends from any other shares you hold.
- Anything you hold inside an ISA grows free of both capital gains tax and dividend tax, and you can pay in up to £20,000 across your ISAs each tax year, as set out on HMRC's Individual Savings Accounts page.
