## The 28 July lead incident, resolved

A lead emailed Simon saying he could not send the contact form. Simon and Emil
assumed Instagram was at fault. It was not.

**Root cause, three failures stacked:**

1. `RESEND_API_KEY` had been rotated out from under the site and returned HTTP 401
   on every send. Lead notifications AND the welcome sequences had been silently
   failing for roughly a month.
2. The FormSubmit fallback answers HTTP 200 even when it refuses to deliver.
   `notify()` only checked `res.ok`, so that path was broken and misreported.
3. The route gated its response solely on the email send, so it returned 502 and
   told the lead to go away, even though the Supabase row had stored fine.

**Resolution:** new Resend key live in Vercel Production and `.env.local`,
verified end to end with a real submission through the live form (notification
and welcome email both reached `last_event: delivered`, test data then purged).
The route now returns success when either the email or the row succeeded, parses
the FormSubmit body, and logs every failure reason including the previously
invisible stored-but-not-emailed case.

**`srjinternational.co.uk` is verified in Resend.** The domain was never the
problem.

## HMRC Timely Payments: verified research

Consultation "Timely Payments in income tax Self Assessment", ran 23 June to
4 August 2026. Every figure below was confirmed against an exact quote in the
consultation document. All cited gov.uk URLs returned 200 on 29 July 2026.

- From April 2029, Self Assessment taxpayers with "sufficient PAYE income" pay
  their SA liability through PAYE each pay period, in equal payments across the
  year, forecast from their last filed ITSA return and updatable. Around
  **2.1 million** of the approximately **7 million** who have both ITSA and PAYE
  income are in scope.
- Tax collected through PAYE in any pay period stays **capped at 50% of PAYE
  income**.
- **9.5 million** Self Assessment filers do not meet the criteria. Around
  **2.5 million** of those make payments on account today. For this group HMRC
  "would like to explore" **monthly or quarterly** payments on account, also from
  April 2029. This is explicitly exploratory, **not announced policy**.
- **Question 21** asks about reducing the **£1,000** payments on account threshold.
  Current rule: liabilities over £1,000 with less than 80% deducted at source
  means two equal payments on account.
- **Transition year 2029-30**: the remaining 2028-29 liability is paid under the
  current arrangements *alongside* forecast 2029-30 liabilities. The consultation
  asks what support is needed, floating a Budget Payment Plan and spreading.
- 27 consultation questions in total.

**The angle.** The document never quantifies "sufficient PAYE income". That
undefined phrase alone decides whether an agency founder on a small director's
salary plus dividends lands in the PAYE regime or the payments-on-account regime.
That gap is the reason the page would be worth reading, and it was a legitimate
reason for a CTA to respond.
